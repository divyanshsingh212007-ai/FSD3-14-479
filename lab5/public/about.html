About Gambit House

A chess server that
answers to its players.

We started in a Rotterdam flat in 2013 because the club had nowhere to play on a Tuesday. Thirteen years later the servers are bigger and the rule is the same: everything free, nothing sold, no one's game turned into an advertisement.

Why we exist

Chess is roughly fifteen hundred years old and belongs to nobody. That seemed like a poor match for a game locked behind a subscription, with an engine that only thinks properly once you've paid, and a puzzle counter that stops at five a day.

So Gambit House was built the other way round. Every feature on the site is available to every account from the first minute: full-depth analysis, unlimited puzzles, the complete opening database, tournament hosting, broadcasts, coaching tools. There is no premium tier to upgrade to, because there is nothing behind it.

If a feature is worth building, it is worth giving to everyone who plays here.

How it's paid for

Players donate. That's the whole model. Roughly one in four hundred accounts gives something in a year, which covers the servers, the engine farm, the fair-play team and eleven salaries. We publish the accounts every January, down to the line item, and the code has been open source since the first commit.

We don't run advertising, we don't sell analytics about your games, and we have turned down acquisition offers three times. The nonprofit foundation that owns the site cannot be sold; that was written into its statutes in 2016 specifically so that this sentence would still be true today.

On fair play

An engine can beat any human alive, which makes cheating the one thing that could genuinely kill an online chess server. Every rated game is screened, every arena is reviewed before trophies are awarded, and appeals are read by people rather than a model. We would rather reverse a result late than leave a false one standing.

The moves so far

Thirteen years, condensed. The years that mattered were rarely the ones with the biggest numbers.

2013
A flat in Rotterdam

Written over one winter so the Kingside club had somewhere to play between Tuesdays. Fourteen members, one server under a desk.

2015
Arenas arrive

Anyone could now run a tournament without asking us first. Within a month players were hosting more events per week than we had held in the previous two years.

2016
The foundation

Ownership moved to a nonprofit whose statutes forbid sale, advertising and paid features. Three acquisition approaches have been declined since.

2019
Fair play team

Screening moved from a script to a standing team of eight, with a published appeals process and a public record of reversed results.

2021
Ten million games in a day

The site fell over twice during the boom. The post-mortems are still online, unedited, including the embarrassing one about the database index.

2024
Broadcasts and clubs

Over-the-board events started relaying through us from DGT boards, and clubs got their own pages, ladders and calendars.

2026
Ninety-six arenas at once

Almost all of them run by players we have never met, which is the part we're proudest of.

What we promise

Four commitments written into the foundation's statutes. Changing any of them needs a vote of the members, not a decision by us.

  • ●
    Free means free

    No premium tier, no feature limits, no move caps, no engine depth held back for paying accounts.

  • ●
    Your games are yours

    Export everything you've ever played as PGN, at any time. We don't sell game data or behavioural analytics to anyone.

  • ●
    Open books, open source

    Annual accounts published every January, line by line. The entire codebase is public and licensed for reuse.

  • ●
    Not for sale

    The foundation cannot be acquired. If Gambit House ever shuts down, the code and the database stay public.
